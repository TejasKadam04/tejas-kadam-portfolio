About the Dispatcher

Driver's log

I got my first professional experience early — interning at ICICI Bank during my second year of a Bachelor's in Management Studies. I spent that year learning how a bank actually runs day to day: sitting with corporate culture, facing customers directly, closing loan applications, and solving the kind of problems and queries that come at you from a dozen people in a single afternoon.

It was solid experience, but I wanted to build something different from the path everyone around me was taking. That's when logistics found me — my brother-in-law opened the door to Freight Sector, a Canada-based company whose sister operation, Coldcalls, ran the India side of the business. I started there on clerical work and cold-calling to bring in new customers and report back to stakeholders, all remote from India. It taught me how the industry actually works in Canada before I'd ever set foot here.

While I kept sharpening those skills with Freight Sector, I was already planning the next move: a Master's in Canada, to get the exposure I couldn't get from a distance. I stayed with the company through that stretch and went deep on the trucking side of the business along the way.

After finishing my MBA, Protrans Personnel Services placed me as a Fleet Coordinator and Dispatcher at Penske Logistics. I took the contract role immediately — it was exactly the kind of hands-on exposure to local transportation I'd been chasing, and it turned into one of the best decisions I've made in this career: I was thrown straight into the deep end of dispatch and came out knowing the local freight world inside out.

When that contract ran its course, I moved to Brimich Logistics — a warehousing and transportation company — as a Transportation Dispatcher in their transportation department, coordinating loads and keeping fleet movement on schedule. I'm ambitious about where this goes next, and I'm looking to stretch further into operations, purchasing, and analytics.

Trained in India. Built the career in Canada. Still building.
